### How I Got Into Web Development

My first website that was seen by the public was a fan site and download center for the PC game series <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RollerCoaster_Tycoon" target="_blank">RollerCoaster Tycoon</a>. Called **RCT Market** and launched originally on GeoCities sometime around 2001, it first appears in the Archive.org Wayback Machine <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20020426014315/http://rctmarket.rctoa.com/" target="_blank">in April of 2002</a> (v2, a custom PHP incarnation). The experience of building and managing both a site and a community was a fantastic opportunity for a 12-13 year old, and helped set me along towards my future career choice.

After RCT Market, I focused mostly on school and playing soccer for quite a few years. One semi-successful website I managed during this time was a general purpose gaming hub called GamingChamber.com. Gaming Chamber was, at its peak, a community of around 200 users who discussed console and PC gaming, and also regularly joined up to play with/against each other in online games. One of the final initiatives of the site staff before the site folded was a short-lived, two-issue "magazine" for the handheld Sony PSP called I^2 (Industry Insider), one of the first (and in retrospect, last) of its kind.

During my last year of high school in Columbus, Ohio and into my first year of college at Ohio University in Athens, I reached out to a couple of guys from England who were already working on the PC Gaming-focused website _De-Frag.com_. They seemed like they had a lot of great content, but could use a site administrator/developer, so I offered them my services and we worked together for close to two years. I also contributed to the writing team for both news and reviews, a similar 'Programmer/Writer' role to those I performed on both RCT Market and Gaming Chamber.

### What I Do for a Living Now

Starting in 2013 I became a professional web developer for <a href="http://www.ev.net/" target="_blank">Electronic Vision</a>, a multimedia company in Athens with a long and rich history of being the most cutting-edge shop in the area. A large portion of my projects for EV are full web rebuilds for clients in one of a number of platforms including Wordpress, CommonSpot, Moodle, MantisBT, Drupal, and more.

My development environment is a Windows 7 machine with an AMD FX-8350, 16GB of RAM, and a triple wide-screen monitor setup. I use Cygwin to emulate a lot of *nix functions in <a href="https://github.com/Maximus5/ConEmu" target="_blank">ConEmu</a>, and also use VMWare Workstation and VirtualBox to emulate a large number of other platforms.
